RESEARCH NOTES · VERSION 0.1

Methodology & limitations

For research and education. Not financial advice. Correlation is not causation. Historical reactions do not predict future returns.

Exposure is a proximity screen

For each event we find headquarters within a configurable radius. Defaults: earthquake 150 km, fire 50 km, severe weather 300 km, strike 75 km, port closure 120 km, protest 30 km. Haversine distances use a 6,371 km Earth radius. Facility scores are weight × max(0, 1 − distance / radius), summed per company and capped at one. Unknown weights use a conservative 0.2 proxy. Scores are low below 0.3, moderate below 0.6, high otherwise. Confidence measures data quality separately.

The initial 50-company dataset contains headquarters, not a comprehensive operating or supply-chain footprint. Coordinates are approximate. Each record includes corporate evidence links and review status. An unreviewed corporate URL is a lead, not verified facility evidence. No match does not mean no exposure.

Market-model event study

Daily simple returns are close(t) / close(t−1) − 1, using adjusted closes for both company and SPY benchmark. Fit r(company) = alpha + beta × r(SPY) + error by ordinary least squares over trading days −140 through −21 (120 returns). Estimate abnormal returns as actual minus expected returns. Sum them to calculate cumulative abnormal returns (CAR) over [−1,+1], [0,+5], and [0,+20]. CAR is a sum, not a compounded portfolio return.

Day zero is the first supplied benchmark date on or after the UTC event date. After-market-close timing is not modeled. Require complete company observations on all benchmark dates from −141 through +20, at least 120 estimation returns, a varying benchmark, and matching synthetic/observed status. Reject unexplained benchmark gaps longer than seven days. This is not a complete exchange-calendar audit.

Historical aggregation and uncertainty

Use earlier events of the same type and coarse region (US West west of −114°, Central west of −95°, East otherwise, within the contiguous-US bounding rectangle). Require 42 calendar days between selected events to reduce overlap. Average company CARs within each event, then equally average event means. Company observations are reported separately from the number of events.

Standard error is the sample standard deviation of event means divided by sqrt(n). Two-sided 95% Student-t confidence intervals use conservative tabulated critical values. At least five events and nonzero estimated variability are necessary to show exploratory significance. This is a display threshold, not a guarantee of statistical power. Intervals assume independence, are not adjusted for multiple testing, and do not establish causality.

Source caveats

USGS earthquakes are observed point records. NWS alert polygons become approximate vertex centroids; alerts without geometry are omitted. Broad hazard geometry is not intersected with facilities. NASA FIRMS thermal anomalies are not confirmed fire damage; severity 2 is an unknown-impact display default. GDELT point records are unverified news mentions: search terms do not prove event type, retrieval timestamps do not identify incident time, and these records are excluded from studies. News HTML is retained as raw provenance but never rendered.

Illustrative mode uses fictional events and deterministic synthetic prices on a weekday calendar that includes holidays. These are not market observations. Observed price data must be cached separately. Alpha Vantage free compact daily data is unadjusted and usually too short for these studies; it is suitable for a labeled chart, not this analytics method. Import licensed adjusted closes for companies and SPY to calculate observed studies.

Limits of inference

Current-company survivorship, HQ-only coverage, selection bias, uncertain geocoding, coarse regions, after-close timing, overlapping or correlated events, unrelated earnings/news, split/dividend adjustment errors, and incomplete histories can dominate results. Studies are descriptive research tools. Neither proximity nor historical CAR should be treated as a prediction or a recommendation.
